Biguli Secondary School

Founded in 1999, Biguli Secondary School has served learners and families through education shaped by community, faith and the hope of a brighter future. As a church-founded school operating within Uganda’s Universal Secondary Education (USE/UPS as locally described) context, the school’s journey brings together access to education, values and academic ambition.

This page highlights the school’s beginnings and the leadership period of Headteacher Katumire Elias, who, according to the school history supplied for this page, became headteacher in 2009.

Top Researchers

Preserving and sharing the school story

Building opportunity for learners

Biguli Secondary School began in 1999. Its identity as a church-founded school is an important part of its story, connecting education with service, responsibility and moral development. As a school serving learners through the universal secondary education programme, it has an important role in widening access to secondary education.

Over the years, the school’s aspirations have included helping learners gain knowledge, develop discipline, participate in school life and prepare for further education and responsible adulthood.

A school’s legacy is built over time through the commitment of learners, teachers, families, school leaders and the community.
LEADERSHIP AND PROGRESS

Katumire Elias Becomes Headteacher — 2009

A chapter focused on academic ambition and learner success

Raising expectations for achievement

According to the school history provided for this page, Katumire Elias became headteacher of Biguli Secondary School in 2009. His leadership period is remembered by the school community as an important stage in its academic journey.

From that period, students were reported to have begun performing strongly enough to qualify for further study at private and other post-secondary institutions. This progress was especially meaningful for a school serving learners under a universal secondary education programme and founded on church values.

The story reflects an ambition that learners should not be limited by the school’s funding model or background: they should be encouraged to work hard, perform well in examinations and pursue further opportunities.
